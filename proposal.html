Problem Statement 01
Prevent Critical Knowledge
From Leaving With People

Continuum

Project handovers that show their work.
Continuum rebuilds the current state of a project from its repository, team chat, notes and task board, turns it into a handover where every statement points to its source, asks the departing member only the questions the evidence cannot answer, and lets a reviewer approve exactly what the successor is allowed to see.

Round 1 Proposal (PDF)
At a glance

The proposal in one page

When someone leaves a small software team, the code stays but the reasons, the half-finished work and the “ask Aditi, she knows” knowledge leave with them. Handover notes are usually written in the last few days, from memory, by the person with the least time left. We think that order is backwards.

What we are solving

The handover of an active software project in a small team (3–25 people) whose work lives in a Git repository, a team chat, shared notes and a task board — from the day a departure is known until the successor’s first productive week. Primary setting: student tech clubs and college project teams, where turnover happens every semester.

What we will build

A web app that follows the required Connect → Reconstruct → Verify → Review → Handover flow and produces a role-scoped handover: current state, decisions and their reasons, owners, risks, dependencies, contacts and a first-week plan — with a citation on every line and an honest label wherever evidence is thin, missing or contradictory.

Four ideas that make Continuum different

IdeaWhat it means in practice
1. Claims, not summariesThe handover is built from atomic claims. Each carries an evidence state — Verified Inferred Conflicting Missing — and one or more source links. Nothing is presented as fact without a source.
2. Gap InterviewInstead of asking the leaver to write a document from scratch, Continuum asks them a short list of targeted questions only where evidence is missing or contradictory. Their answers become new, citable evidence.
3. Permission before generationAccess rules are applied before any text reaches the AI model. A successor’s view is generated only from sources that person is cleared to see, so the model cannot leak what it was never given.
4. Handover as a loopThe successor acknowledges each section or marks it “unclear”. Unclear items go back to the reviewer and leaver while the leaver is still reachable — the notice period becomes a working window, not a deadline.

§2 · Understanding of the problem

Knowledge does not disappear. It was never in one place.

Most teams do have documents. The problem is that the knowledge a successor actually needs is not in them — it is spread across commit messages, PR review threads, late-night chat discussions, meeting notes and the leaver’s memory.

What exactly is lost when someone leaves

Type of knowledgeWhere it usually livesWhat happens without it
Reasons behind decisionsPR review comments, chat threads, a meeting note no one links toThe successor reverses a decision that was made for a good reason, and repeats the same mistake.
True state of unfinished workOpen PRs, stale branches, TODOs, “almost done” tasksWork marked 90% done turns out to be 40% done; deadlines slip.
Dependencies and accessEnvironment configs, third-party dashboards, who owns which credentialProduction breaks and nobody knows who holds the account.
Recurring problems and workaroundsChat (“restart the bot again”), issue comments, the leaver’s habitsKnown issues are rediscovered from scratch, often under pressure.
People and contactsDMs, email, the leaver’s phoneThe successor doesn’t know who to ask — the sponsor, the faculty coordinator, the API vendor.

Why existing documentation fails

1It records the what, not the why

A README explains how to run the project. The reason payments moved to UPI links is in a chat thread from August.

2It drifts out of date

Docs are written once and code keeps changing. The README says “deployed on Render”; the last three commits configure Vercel.

3The handover note is written at the worst time

In the last week, from memory, by a person with exams, a new job or simply less motivation. It covers what they remember, not what matters.

4The successor doesn’t know what to ask

Unknown unknowns. They discover the gap only when something breaks — usually after the leaver has stopped replying.

Why a plain AI summary is not the answer either

Pointing a language model at a chat export produces fluent text, but it mixes fact with guesswork, cannot say where a statement came from, quietly picks a side when sources disagree, and happily repeats something from a private channel. For a handover — which people will act on — those are exactly the failures that matter. This is why the problem statement insists on traceability, uncertainty marking, human approval and permission awareness, and why we treat those as the core of the product rather than add-ons.

How we framed the problem

A handover has three jobs: tell the truth about the project’s state (traceable), tell the successor what to do next (actionable), and tell them only what they may know (permission-aware). These map one-to-one to the “Success Looks Like” criteria in the brief, and every feature in §6 serves at least one of them.

Three departure scenarios we design for

Graduating club lead

A final-year student hands the club’s event app to a second-year. Time: two to three weeks before exams. Sources: GitHub, Discord, Google Docs. Sensitive: sponsor contacts, payment keys.

Intern leaving mid-project

A six-month intern leaves a feature half-built. Time: notice period of a week or two. Sources: repo, Slack, task board. Sensitive: client names, internal channels.

Volunteer stepping down

A maintainer of a community or NGO tool rotates out. Often no formal notice. Sources: public repo, issue tracker, chat. Sensitive: donor or member data.

Why we start with student tech teams

  • Turnover is guaranteed and predictable. Every semester and every graduating batch triggers handovers, so the problem recurs on a schedule.
  • The sources are standard and exportable. GitHub, Discord or Slack, and Google Docs/Notion all offer APIs or exports — no unrealistic enterprise access is needed.
  • We are the users. We work in this environment, can test with real club teams, and can build realistic synthetic data without touching confidential company material.
  • It generalises. The same workflow applies to startups, research groups and volunteer teams; only the connectors and role labels change.

Evidence and validation

Our Round 1 understanding comes from working inside student project teams and from the brief itself, so we treat it as a hypothesis, not proof. Before and during Round 2 we will run short structured interviews with at least five club leads, mentors or maintainers (what was lost in their last handover, how long the successor took to become productive, what they would never share with a successor) and a handover test: one person takes over a seeded demo project with and without Continuum. Findings and any resulting changes will be reported in the Round 2 document.

The specific part of the problem we solve — and what we leave out

In scope: reconstructing project state from authorised sources, evidence-linked claims with uncertainty labels, a gap interview with the leaver, human review and approval, and a role-scoped handover with acknowledgement. Out of scope for this event: HR offboarding, company-wide knowledge bases, live meeting transcription, and live OAuth connectors for every chat tool — we use official exports instead (see §6).

§4 · Proposed solution

Continuum: a handover that shows its work

Continuum turns scattered project activity into a reviewed, role-appropriate handover. It does three things a document or a chatbot cannot: it keeps every statement tied to evidence, it asks the leaver only about the gaps, and it filters by permission before generating anything.

The core unit: an evidence-backed claim

All imported material is broken into evidence units — one commit, one PR comment, one chat thread, one paragraph of a note. The AI extracts claims from them (a decision, an owner, a risk, a dependency, a next action), and every claim carries its evidence state:

StateMeaningHow it is shown to people
VerifiedAt least one source states it directly, and an automated check confirms the source supports it.Normal text with source chips.
InferredA reasonable conclusion drawn from sources, but no source says it outright.Labelled “AI inference — not stated in any source”.
ConflictingTwo or more sources disagree.Both versions shown side by side. Never auto-resolved.
MissingSomething a handover needs has no evidence at all (for example, an open PR with no owner).Listed as a gap; turned into a Gap Interview question.
ConfirmedA human reviewer approved or corrected the claim.Shows who confirmed it and when.

What the successor receives

  1. Project in five lines — goal, users, stack, current phase.
  2. Current state by workstream — done, in progress, blocked, with the real status of open work.
  3. Decision log — a timeline of key decisions and why they were made.
  4. Your first week — ordered next actions, each linked to its issue, PR or thread.
  1. Risks and landmines — recurring problems, workarounds, fragile parts.
  2. Dependencies and people — services, credential owners, who to ask for what.
  3. Still unresolved — open questions and conflicts the team could not settle, stated honestly.

The Gap Interview

After reconstruction, Continuum knows what it could not find. It turns each Missing or Conflicting item into a short, specific question for the leaver, for example:

“PR #91 ‘temporary fix for login loop’ was merged with a TODO on 18 Sep. Is this still temporary, and what would the proper fix be?”

“The README says production runs on Render, but commits since 2 Oct configure Vercel. Which is live today?”

“Who owns the college SMTP account used for event emails? No source mentions it.”

The leaver answers in a few minutes per question, and each answer is stored as a new evidence unit — cited as Exit Q&A · Aditi · 3 Nov — so even their personal knowledge becomes traceable. A typical handover should need a short list of questions, not a blank document.

§5 · Complete product workflow

From connected sources to an approved handover

The workflow follows the five stages required by the brief. Each stage has a clear owner and a clear output.

StageWhat the user doesWhat Continuum does
1 · ConnectReviewer creates a handover (leaver, successor, successor’s role, target date), connects a GitHub repo with a read-only token, and uploads a Slack/Discord export, notes and a task-board export. Chooses which channels and folders are in scope.Parses everything into evidence units, scans for secrets and personal data, and attaches an access label from the source (e.g. #core-team → leads).
2 · ReconstructWaits a few minutes; can watch progress per source.Groups evidence by thread and time window, extracts claims, builds the decision timeline, ownership map, risk list, dependency list and next actions.

§8 · Major technical challenges

The hard parts, and how we handle them

Challenge 1 — Making every claim trustworthy (the main one)

Language models produce confident text that may not be supported. A handover that invents a reason for a decision is worse than no handover. We do not rely on prompting alone; we wrap the model in checks we control.

#StepHow it works
1Bounded inputThe model sees one batch at a time (a thread or a time window), with every evidence unit tagged by a stable ID.
2Schema-bound extractionOutput must be JSON matching our claim schema: type, statement, cited evidence IDs and the exact quote that supports it. Free text is rejected.
3Deterministic checksEvery cited ID must exist and must have been in that batch; the quoted span must fuzzy-match the source text (rapidfuzz). Failures are dropped or downgraded to Inferred.
4Support checkA second, narrow model call sees only the claim and its cited evidence and answers: supports / partially / does not. Only “supports” can become Verified.
5Human confirmationNothing is published until a reviewer approves it; reviewer edits are stored as Confirmed with name and time.

Challenge 2 — Not leaking what the successor shouldn’t see

If the model reads a private channel while writing a contributor’s handover, the information can surface in a summary even if the channel is “hidden” in the UI. So we filter before generation:

  • Each evidence unit inherits an access label from its source (e.g. #core-team → leads, finance/ → restricted).
  • A handover view is generated only from units the target role may read. Postgres row-level security enforces this at query level, so a bug in the app layer cannot bypass it.
  • A claim takes the most restrictive label of its evidence. If the successor cannot see all of its evidence, they do not see the claim.
  • Secrets (API keys, tokens) and personal data are detected and redacted at ingestion; reviewers can redact any span manually before publishing.

Challenge 3 — Contradictory and stale information

Sources disagree all the time. Claims about the same subject are grouped using embeddings and an entity key (module, service, person); each group is checked for contradiction. Conflicts are shown with both sides and their dates — the newest source is not assumed to be correct. Publishing is blocked until each conflict is resolved or explicitly marked unresolved.

Challenge 4 — Long, noisy input and cost

A semester of chat can be thousands of messages of jokes, memes and off-topic talk. We filter obvious noise cheaply (bots, reactions, very short messages), thread messages before extraction, cache results per batch so re-runs only process new evidence, and cap batch size so cost stays predictable on student credits. If the AI API fails or rate-limits, jobs retry with back-off and the UI shows which sources are incomplete instead of producing a partial handover silently.

§9 · Trust, privacy and human control

How we meet the brief’s trust requirements

Requirement in the briefHow Continuum meets it
Do not present generated assumptions as verified organisational truth.Five explicit evidence states; inferences labelled in the text itself; only checked claims can be “Verified”; reviewer confirmation is recorded separately from AI verification.
Do not expose confidential information merely because it appeared in a connected source.Source-level scoping at connect time, access labels, permission filtering before generation, row-level security, secret/PII redaction, reviewer “mark sensitive”.
Allow authorised users to correct, reject and approve the final handover.Claim-level approve / edit / reject / redact, version history, publish lock, and an audit log of every action.
Use synthetic, public, permissioned or anonymised data for demonstrations.The demo runs on our synthetic “CampusKart” project only. The product asks the admin to confirm they are authorised to connect each source.

Data handling

  • Read-only access to every source; Continuum never writes back to GitHub or chat.
  • Only the excerpts needed for a batch are sent to the AI API, after redaction.
  • Tokens and uploaded files are stored encrypted and scoped to one workspace.
  • Deleting a handover deletes its evidence units, embeddings and files.
  • The leaver can see what the system extracted about their work before it is shared.
  • Accessibility: keyboard navigation and text labels on every status — colour is never the only signal.

Honest limitations we will state in the product

Continuum can only reconstruct what was written down somewhere. Knowledge that lives purely in someone’s head reaches the handover only through the Gap Interview, and a reviewer still has to judge whether the answers are right. We would rather show a clear “Missing” than fill the gap with a plausible guess.

§13 · Future scope

After the event

Always-on handover readiness

Run continuously, not only at departure. Show each team which modules depend on a single person (the “bus factor”) long before anyone leaves.

Live connectors

OAuth sync for Slack, Discord, Notion, Google Drive, Jira and Linear, respecting each tool’s own permissions automatically.

Successor Q&A

Let the successor ask questions in plain language, answered only from approved, permitted evidence with citations.

Self-hosted and local-model option

For organisations that cannot send data to an external API: on-premise deployment with an open-weight model.

Templates by setting

Handover templates for research groups (datasets, experiments), NGOs (donors, volunteers) and startups (customers, infra).

Club and college programme

Offer Continuum to college tech clubs as an end-of-semester handover tool — our first real users and a path to adoption.
