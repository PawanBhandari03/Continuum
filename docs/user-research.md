# User research

Status: **survey run, 6 responses** (collected 9 Nov 2026 via the Google Form below). Interviews: not yet run. Figures and quotes below are copied from the form's response summary; names are withheld. No invented people, quotes or numbers.

## Method
- **Survey (Google Form):** https://docs.google.com/forms/d/e/1FAIpQLScWrSStyRK5dNmzR1mL0Tefdtam-_ZutNAd2K4Ov_npCldLAA/viewform
  Target: at least 5 responses from club leads, mentors, members, or people who have handed a project over. Anonymous by default; quotes used only where the respondent answered "Yes" to "May we quote you anonymously?".
- **Short interviews (optional, 10–15 min):** same questions, in person or on a call. Respondents were told we use only fake demo data and not to share anything confidential.
- Raw responses are kept in the linked Google Sheet (not committed, to protect respondents).

## Questions asked
1. Role · 2. Team size · 3. How often people leave or join
4. How does a handover happen today? · 5. What was lost last time, and how did you find out? · 6. What kind of knowledge is lost most?
7. Would you trust a handover written by software (1–5)? · 8. What would you need to see first? · 9. Would you approve each statement before the new person sees it? · 10. What should never be shown?
11. (Optional) Anything confusing or missing in the demo? · 12. May we quote you anonymously?

## Responses summary (6 responses, all copied from the form)
| Question | Result |
|---|---|
| Role | Club/team lead 2 · Developer/member 3 · Handed over a project before 1 · Mentor 0 · Other 0 |
| Team size | 1–5: 3 · 6–15: 2 · 16–30: 1 · 30+: 0 |
| How often people leave or join | Every semester 2 · Rarely 3 · Not sure 1 · Every year 0 |
| How handover happens today (multi-select) | Nothing formal 3 · Call/meeting 3 · Shared drive/GitHub README 2 · Document/notes 1 · Chat messages 1 · Other 1 |
| Knowledge lost most (multi-select) | Why decisions were made 4 · Who owns what 4 · Unfinished work 3 · Recurring bugs 3 · Passwords and access 2 · Who to ask 2 |
| Trust a software-written handover (1–5) | 3: 2 people · 4: 4 people · 1, 2 and 5: 0. Average 3.7 (calculated from these counts). Nobody chose 5 (full trust). |
| Needed before trusting (multi-select) | Links to original sources 3 · Marked uncertain or missing parts 3 · Ability to correct it 3 · Who approved it 2 |
| Approve each statement before the new person sees it | "Yes, if it takes under 10 minutes" 5 · "Yes" 1 · "No, too slow" 0 · Not sure 0 |
| May we quote you anonymously | Yes 6 · No 0 |

What this suggests (small sample, not proof): people lose *reasons for decisions* and *ownership* most, which is what Continuum's decision log and owner/gap claims target. Nobody gave full trust, and most wanted visible sources, flagged uncertainty and the ability to correct, which matches the evidence states and review step. Reviewing claims was acceptable to everyone if quick, so the "approve all Verified" and "approve all remaining" shortcuts matter.

## What we changed because of the research
| Finding | Change | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Demo feedback said "confusing" and "yes, make it easy" | First-run guided tour (8 steps) with a Help button; "Approve all remaining" shortcut on the ledger. The tour was already planned, so this feedback supports it rather than causing it. | Built (commit `8dd9e3d`). Not re-tested with respondents. |
| Respondents named passwords, IDs and database access as never-to-show | Already in the design: secret/PII redaction and lead-only labels; Contributor view excludes lead-only content (tested on the mock API). | Existing feature; real API test pending |
| Most wanted visible sources and uncertainty | Already in the design: source chips with the original text, evidence-state badges. | Existing feature |

## Limits
Six responses from a non-random sample (friends and club contacts), so it shows reactions, not statistical proof. Respondent independence was not verified: some respondents may personally know the team. The demo-feedback question gave mostly one-word answers, so it tells us little about *what* was confusing. No in-person interviews have been run yet.
